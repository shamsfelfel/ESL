## What the test is for

It is a **progress test against this course**, not a TOEFL score. It says
whether Lessons 4 to 8 have stuck and which lesson to go back to. Test 1's own
key says so in as many words, and yours must keep that paragraph's sense.

**Both texts must be ones she has never read.** Write two new ones. Nothing in
this test may be answerable from memory of a lesson, so do not reuse a
passage, a character or a situation from Lessons 1 to 12. New people, new
places.

## The five sections — the mark split does not change

| Section | Marks | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Listening | 20 | 20 |
| 2 Reading | 30 | 25 |
| 3 Vocabulary and grammar | 30 | 20 |
| 4 Writing | 10 | 15 |
| 5 Speaking | 10 | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** | about 90 |

Six parts, numbered 01 to 06, slugs exactly: `listening`, `reading`,
`vocabulary-and-grammar`, `writing`, `speaking`, `answer-key`. Parts 01 and 05
have audio; `full_section` is `["01", "05"]`, as in Test 1.

### Section 1 — Listening, 20 marks

A spoken text of **about 300 words** she has never heard, then five
multiple-choice questions at 3 marks and one short answer at 5. Label them
`**1. (Factual, 3 marks)**` and so on, exactly as Test 1 does. The text is read
once at normal pace and then once more, with the narrator saying so — follow
Test 1's segments for how that is staged, and leave real seconds of silence to
answer in.

### Section 2 — Reading, 30 marks

**This is the section that grows.** Test 1's passage is 241 words, and its own
key promises her that *full-length sections and a far longer reading passage*
come after Lesson 8 — which is this test. She has been reading 530-word
passages every week since Lesson 6, so:

- the passage is **540 to 600 words**, with its count printed in the heading as
  `### Passage: *Title* (NNN words)` and set in `passage_words`;
- eight questions, using **all eight TOEFL types** the lessons teach, at 4 and
  3 marks to total 30 — Test 1 uses six at 4 and two at 3;
- the four insertion markers `[A] [B] [C] [D]` in the passage, in `print_only`,
  printed and never spoken.

The eight types, complete since Lesson 5: Factual, Negative factual, Vocabulary
in context, Reference, Inference, Sentence insertion, Purpose of a paragraph,
Purpose of the whole passage, The writer's attitude. Nine names for eight
slots, so one of them sits out — Test 1 doubles Factual and leaves the later
types to the lessons that teach them. Choose eight that cover Lessons 4 to 8
and say in the progress file which you left out and why.

A text that **argues** rather than only narrates, because that is what Lessons 7
and 8 taught her to read.

### Section 3 — Vocabulary and grammar, 30 marks

Four parts, as in Test 1, and this is where Lessons 4 to 8 are actually
examined:

- **Part A — Vocabulary, 10 marks, 1 each.** One word per gap, defined the way
  Test 1 defines them (*A second copy of your files in another place is a
  \_\_\_\_*). Every answer must be one of the **70 key words taught in Lessons
  4 to 8** — `python scripts/course_state.py` lists them per lesson. Spread the
  ten across all five lessons; do not take six from one.
- **Part B — Grammar, 10 marks, 1 each.** The five lessons' five structures,
  two gaps each: present perfect with *for*, *since*, *yet*, *already*
  (Lesson 4); present perfect continuous, including a state verb that must not
  take it (Lesson 5); the future, *will* against *going to*, and the present
  simple in a time clause after *when* or *as soon as* (Lesson 6); the modals
  — *can*, *could*, *should*, *must*, *have to*, and *mustn't* against *don't
  have to* (Lesson 7); the first and second conditional, and no *will* in the
  *if*-clause (Lesson 8).
- **Part C — Sound, 5 marks, 1 each.** Test 1's Part C is the three sounds of
  `-ed`, which is Lesson 2's pronunciation point. Yours is the contractions
  and weak forms those five lessons taught: `'ve` and `'s` (Lesson 4), the
  weak *been* (Lesson 5), `'ll` and *gonna* (Lesson 6), *can* against *can't*
  and *hafta* (Lesson 7), `'d` (Lesson 8). It must be answerable **on paper,
  with no audio**, because this part is on the sheet — ask her to write the
  short form, or which of two words is the strong one, not to identify a sound
  she cannot hear.
- **Part D — Which word is different? 5 marks, 1 each.** Five rows of four
  words from Lessons 4 to 8, each row with one that does not belong for a
  reason the key states.

### Section 4 — Writing, 10 marks

One task, with the mark table on the sheet, as Test 1 has it. Make it need the
tenses: something she has been doing (present perfect continuous), something
she has finished (present perfect), what she will do next (future), and one
conditional. Sixty to eighty words — not an essay.

### Section 5 — Speaking, 10 marks

Prompts with audio, and a mark table on the sheet. Two or three prompts, each
naming the structure it is listening for, in Test 1's manner.

### Part 06 — the answer key

Last part, and **`"in_whole": false`** — that is how the whole test prints
without printing its own answers, and it is the single most important line in
the file. Check how Test 1 sets it before you write it.

It carries, in this order:

1. A marking table per section, question by question, with the marks.
2. A sentence saying **why** the sentence-insertion answer is right and why the
   tempting wrong one is wrong, as Test 1 does for its questions 7 and 8.
3. Answers for Part A to Part D, in Test 1's `1. flat · 2. backup · …` form,
   with Part D giving the reason for each row.
4. `### What the score means {: #what-the-score-means }` — a score band table.
   Test 1's bands send her to Lesson 4; yours send her to Lesson 9, and the
   bottom band repeats the weakest of Lessons 4 to 8 rather than all five.
   Keep the paragraph that says this is a progress test against this course and
   not a TOEFL score — and update its last sentence, which in Test 1 promises
   full-length sections after Lesson 8: that promise is being kept here, so say
   what is true now, which is that a real TOEFL estimate still needs a longer
   listening section and a second passage.
5. `### Which lesson to go back to {: #which-lesson }` — each section and part
   mapped to the lesson that fixes it. This is the part that makes it a
   progress test, so map Part B gap by gap, not section by section.
6. `### Teacher notes {: #teacher-notes }`.
