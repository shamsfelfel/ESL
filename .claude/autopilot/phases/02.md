## The passage

525 words. A claim about a scholarship deadline travels through three groups on
a phone: a screenshot of a screenshot, a date that gains a day, a *they said*
that nobody can trace. One student checks the university's own page and finds
the original. A second movement on what checking costs — two minutes and one
page — and why nobody does it: forwarding feels like helping, and the first
person who forwarded it was trying to be useful.

**This is the lesson where invention matters most.** Invent the university, the
groups, the deadline and the date. No real organisation, no real figure, no
real link, nothing lifted from reporting. A passage about how false claims
spread must not itself contain one.

Four insertion markers `[A] [B] [C] [D]` in `print_only`, as in Lesson 8.
Reported speech has to be in the passage, repeatedly, before part 04 names it.

## Part 04 — the grammar

Reported speech, in the order the brief sets out: *say* against *tell*, the
backshift, the time and place shifts, reported questions with the question word
order undone, *if/whether*, and *told me to* / *asked me not to*. Then **Come
back to**: the passive from Lesson 9, expanded into the impersonal reporting
passive — *it is said that*, *it was reported that*, *she is thought to have
left already*.

Open the part with the come-back, and make the link explicit: reported speech
names the source, the reporting passive deletes it. *The deadline was said to
have moved* is fluent, grammatical and tells you nothing about who said it.
That is the same question Lesson 9's part 06 asked of a number, arriving this
time as grammar. Say so in the sheet, and link back to Lesson 9 by name.

The backshift needs the past perfect, which is Lesson 10's. Use it as something
she has, not as something new, and say which lesson it came from.

Teach the trap: the backshift is not automatic — something still true can stay
in the present — and the reported *that* is usually dropped in speech, which is
why she will not hear it.

## Part 05 — the pronunciation

`05-said-told-and-that`. The weak *said*, *told her* run together as
*told'er*, the *that* that disappears, and the stress landing on the claim
rather than the reporting verb. Minimal pairs and a listen-and-choose exercise,
built the way Lesson 10's part 05 is built.

## Part 06 — the comprehension

Ten questions, the eight types, Lesson 8's order and labelling, then Part C
with two extra words. Every distractor wrong for a nameable reason.

This passage gives you the best inference questions in the run — who said it
first, and what the student actually verified — so use them there rather than
spending the inference question on something the text states outright.
