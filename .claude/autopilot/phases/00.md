# Phase 00 — Write Lesson 10, *An Hour Too Late*

Write the lesson. Do not build it: phase 01 does that, with fresh eyes, and the
separation is deliberate — it is how a stale word count gets caught instead of
shipped.

`docs/LESSONS-10-12-BRIEF.md` §"Lesson 10" is your specification. This prompt
is how to execute it.

## What you deliver

1. `lessons/lesson-10/lesson.json` — the whole lesson: twelve parts, their
   printed Markdown, their spoken segments, fourteen key words, five recalled
   from Lesson 8, five games.
2. `lessons/lesson-10/build.py` — copied byte-for-byte from
   `lessons/lesson-09/build.py`. `cp`, nothing else. Verify with `md5sum`.
3. `docs/LESSONS-10-12-PROGRESS.md` — created, with your section.

Nothing else. Not the repository `README.md` (the last phase rewrites it once,
and the guard will refuse you), not `app/catalog.json`, not another lesson.

## The passage

520 words, the number in the brief, and the shape it describes: the accounts of
a small shop, a disk that fails in the morning, a midnight backup whose log
shows it finished an hour too late and copied the damage rather than the
accounts. A second movement about what she changes afterwards. The sentence the
lesson is built towards is that a backup nobody has restored from is not a
backup.

- Written, not reported. No real shop, no real figure, no real organisation.
- Four insertion markers `[A] [B] [C] [D]`, printed in the sheet and never
  spoken, declared in `print_only` on part 01 exactly as Lesson 8 does it.
- The past perfect has to be *in* the passage, naturally and repeatedly, before
  part 04 names it — that is how every grammar point in this course arrives.
  Put at least one third conditional in the second movement.
- Write the title heading as `### Passage: *An Hour Too Late* (520 words)` and
  set `passage_words` to the same number. Count the words. The gate counts them
  too.

## Part 04 — the grammar

The past perfect, taught against the past simple she has had since Lesson 2,
because the only reason the form exists is to put two past actions in order.
Then **Come back to**: the conditionals from Lesson 8, expanded into the third
conditional. Open the part with the come-back, as Lessons 6 to 9 do, so the
spiral is visible to her rather than only to the author.

Teach the trap the brief names: a sequence told in the order it happened needs
no past perfect at all, and three *hads* in a row is worse English than three
past simples. Give her sentences where it is wrong as well as sentences where
it is right.

Include a table of the past participles the passage uses, the irregular ones
marked — she has had five irregulars since Lesson 3 and this is the lesson that
needs them most.

## Part 05 — the pronunciation

`05-had-and-would-have`. The weak *had*, the contracted *'d*, and *would have*
heard as *woulda*. Build it the way Lesson 9's `05-was-were-been` is built:
minimal pairs, a listen-and-choose exercise with the answers in the key, and
segments slow enough to be useful. This part carries more weight than any other
in the lesson, because a third conditional is four words long in speech and she
will hear two of them.

## Part 06 — the comprehension

Ten questions, the eight types, labelled `**1. (Factual)**` and so on, in
Lesson 8's order: Factual, Factual, Negative factual, Vocabulary in context,
Reference, Inference, Sentence insertion, Purpose of a paragraph, Purpose of
the whole passage, The writer's attitude. Then Part C, fill in the blanks, with
two extra words, exactly as Lesson 8 has it.

Every distractor must be wrong for a reason you could name — a plausible
misreading, a word that looks like the answer, a true statement that does not
answer the question. A distractor nobody would choose teaches nothing and makes
the set easier than a real TOEFL set.

## Vocabulary

Fourteen key words, each with `word`, `answer`, `type`, `meaning`, `arabic`,
and `icon` where a drawing exists. The brief's eight anchors are *restore,
overwrite, corrupt, retrieve, snapshot, invoice, delay, confirm*; find the
other six in your own passage. Every one of the fourteen:

- appears in the passage;
- is not one of the 124 already taught — check `--words`, do not trust memory;
- has a meaning written in words she already has, not a dictionary definition;
- has Arabic written as meaning, not as a gloss of the English syntax.

Then five `recall` entries, taken from **Lesson 8's fourteen** and nowhere
else. Prefer the five that fit this passage's subject, since they have to be
heard again inside this lesson, not merely listed.

Icons: prefer a drawing that already exists in `icons.json` — there are 40. You
may add at most two new ones if a concrete key word has none, one colour,
`currentColor`, in the style of the existing set. Never name a drawing that is
not there; `build.py --check` catches it.

## The rest of the parts

02 listening questions, 07 true-or-false, 08 speaking, 09 writing, 10 review,
11 answer key, 12 the passage read slowly. Follow Lesson 8 for all of them,
with the brief's writing task in 09 and the brief's self-report question added
to 10. The answer key is the last part and carries the explanations, including
why each wrong option is wrong.

`full_section` is `01, 02, 06, 07` — comprehension is 06 in a twelve-part
lesson, not 07. Getting that wrong produces a thirty-minute MP3 of the wrong
tracks and the gate checks it.
