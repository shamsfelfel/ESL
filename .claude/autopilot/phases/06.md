# Phase 06 — Sweep the three lessons, then write the course's own account of them

All three lessons exist and are built. This phase decides whether they are
true. It is the only phase that is allowed to find that an earlier one was
wrong, and the only one with all three in front of it.

Do the steps in order; each exposes the next.

    cd ~/work/shams/esl && export PATH="$PWD/.venv/bin:$PATH"
    cat docs/LESSONS-10-12-PROGRESS.md
    python scripts/course_state.py

## 1. The spiral sweep

The course's promise is that no tense is taught once and dropped. Check it as a
fact rather than as an intention:

- Lesson 10 introduces the past perfect and comes back to Lesson 8's
  conditionals, expanded into the third conditional.
- Lesson 11 introduces reported speech and comes back to Lesson 9's passive,
  expanded into the impersonal reporting passive.
- Lesson 12 introduces relative clauses and comes back to Lesson 10's past
  perfect, expanded into the past perfect continuous.

Open each part 04 and confirm the come-back is there, is at the top, and
**expands** rather than repeats. A come-back that only restates the earlier
lesson is repetition, and the README promises a spiral. Say which of the three
is weakest.

## 2. The vocabulary sweep

- Every key word across all twelve lessons is taught exactly once:
  `python scripts/course_state.py --words` ends with `collisions: none`. Paste
  that line.
- Each new lesson's five recalled words come from the lesson two back, and are
  **heard again inside the lesson**, not merely listed on the vocabulary sheet.
  Grep each recalled word in its lesson's other parts. A word that appears only
  in the recall block is not spaced recall; it is a list.
- Every one of the 42 new key words appears in its own passage.

## 3. The number sweep

The word count is printed in three places and only the output blocks
regenerate. For each of the three lessons, check that the count you get by
counting agrees with `passage_words`, with the figure in the passage heading,
and with any figure quoted in the review, the answer key or the lesson's
generated `README.md`. Then check the part count, the minute totals and the
"ten questions" claims the same way.

Report the count of numbers you checked and every disagreement you found.
"Checked" means you counted; it does not mean you read the sentence and it
looked right.

## 4. The duplicate-question sweep

Three lessons' comprehension sets were written in three separate phases that
could not see each other. That is exactly how a course ends up with two
near-identical questions. Compare the ten stems of each new lesson against each
other and against Lessons 6 to 9, and report the highest-similarity pair and
what you did about it. A Jaccard overlap on stems is enough; the point is to
look, not the metric.

Do the same for the true-or-false statements and for Part C's word lists.

## 5. Drive the app

    python make_catalog.py
    python scripts/build-sw.py
    npx playwright test

Then look at it yourself. `python scripts/dev-server.py 8088` serves the app;
open it and confirm:

- all twelve lessons and the test are listed, in order;
- Lessons 10, 11 and 12 each list twelve parts plus five games, each with a
  size shown before it is saved;
- the sizes shown match the files on disk;
- a part saves, and still opens with the network cut off;
- the service worker's cache name starts with `esl-`, and `esl-content-v1` is
  not touched.

## 6. The repository README

This is the one phase allowed to edit `README.md`, and the only one with the
real figures. Update, in the voice the file already has:

- **The lessons table**: three new rows, with level, topic, what is new, the
  real passage word count and `Complete`.
- **The grammar spiral table**: three new rows, introduces and comes-back-to.
- **The "length stops climbing at Lesson 6" section**: it currently says
  Lessons 6 to 9 hold at about 500 and that "Lesson 10 is not written yet".
  Rewrite that paragraph to say what actually happened — seven lessons at one
  length now, the real numbers, and that the question of whether the length
  rises is still hers to answer, with Lesson 12's self-report being where the
  answer comes from.
- **The grammar list in "The question types never change"** and any other
  sentence that counts lessons or says what the course contains.
- **The word-games table and its note**: it says "Lessons 1 to 5 have two games
  each. Lessons 6 to 9 have five." That is now Lessons 6 to 12, and the
  numbering note about Lesson 9's thirteen parts needs the twelve-part lessons
  beside it.
- **The "For the student" link list**: Lessons 10, 11 and 12.
- **Progress Test 2** is still owed, covering Lessons 4 to 8. Say so plainly
  where the tests table is, as the next thing the course needs.

Do not restructure the README. It is a good document; it needs its figures
brought up to date and three rows added.

## 7. The handover

Write `docs/LESSONS-10-12-HANDOVER.md`:

- what the three lessons contain, in counts: words, parts, questions, key
  words, folder sizes, total megabytes added;
- what a human should check first, and in what order;
- what you are least confident about — be specific: a passage that reads
  thinly, a distractor you are unsure of, a pronunciation part you could not
  verify by ear;
- every decision taken under autonomy rule 1, gathered from the progress file;
- what you would do next with another run.

Then note the one thing this run changed that nobody asked for: `make_catalog.py`
rewrote about forty byte figures for Lessons 1 to 9, because those numbers were
last measured on a Windows working copy where the generated HTML carries CRLF.
The Linux figures are the ones GitHub Pages serves, so the new numbers are more
accurate, but Raed will see the diff and should know why it is there. Say that a
`.gitattributes` with `* text=auto eol=lf` would end the churn permanently, and
that it is his call, not yours.
