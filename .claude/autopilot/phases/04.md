## The passage

530 words. A university computer room with a rota on the door, open on the
hours the electricity is on. Describe it through its people and its objects,
because that is what forces the relative clauses: the woman who keeps the list,
the socket that only works if you lean on the plug, the room where everyone
ends up, the student whose laptop charges fastest and who therefore goes last.
Warm and specific; let the non-defining clauses carry the asides.

Second movement: the rota itself — why a list on a door works better than
goodwill, what it costs the person who keeps it, and the small unfairness that
it rewards whoever is nearest the door at eight.

Written, not reported: invent the university and the room. Four insertion
markers in `print_only`. Relative clauses — including at least two
non-defining, and one *which* commenting on a whole clause — must be in the

## Part 04 — the grammar

Relative clauses: defining with *who / which / that / whose / where*, the
object pronoun she can drop, non-defining with its two commas and the rule that
*that* cannot introduce one, and then the B2 use — *which* commenting on a
whole clause: *the generator stopped at eleven, which meant the last hour was
on battery*.

Then **Come back to**: the past perfect from Lesson 10, expanded into the past
perfect continuous — *had been running for three hours* against *had run* —
the length of an action against its completion. Open the part with the
come-back.

Teach the trap: the two commas change the meaning, they do not decorate it.
*The student who keeps the list* picks one student out of many; *the student,
who keeps the list,* says there is one and adds a fact about her. Make her
write both and say what each one claims.

## Part 05 — the pronunciation

`05-commas-and-had-been`. The pause and the falling tone a non-defining clause
is said with, against the unbroken defining clause — the one place in this
course where punctuation is audible, so the audio is the whole lesson here.
And the weak *had been*: *she'd been waiting* is three syllables in speech.

## Part 06 — the comprehension

Ten questions, eight types, Lesson 8's order and labelling, Part C with two
extra words. A description-led passage makes *purpose of a paragraph* and *the
writer's attitude* the interesting ones: the rota paragraph exists to make an
argument, and the attitude towards the woman who keeps the list is the question
worth asking.
