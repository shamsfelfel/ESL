it in the README. You did not write it, which is the point of a separate phase.

## 1. Audit it before you build

- **The marks total 100**, section by section: 20 / 30 / 30 / 10 / 10. Add them
  up yourself, from the question labels, and check each section heading's
  stated total against its own questions.
- **Both texts are new.** Grep the twelve lessons for the names, places and
  situations in them. A text she has already read makes the test worthless, and
  this is the check that cannot be automated away.
- **The reading passage** is 540 to 600 words, and the count in the heading, in
  `passage_words`, and the one you get by counting all agree.
- **Part A's ten answers are all key words from Lessons 4 to 8**, spread across
  the five lessons. `python scripts/course_state.py` lists them.
- **Part B tests all five structures**, and Part C is answerable with no audio.
- **The key is `"in_whole": false`.**
- Every question label carries its marks, and the key answers every question
  that exists — no question without an answer, no answer without a question.

Fix what is wrong in `lesson.json`, re-run `--check`, and report every change.

## 2. Build it

    cd tests/test-02 && python build.py --build

## 3. The check that matters most

**Open `00-lesson.pdf` and confirm the answer key is not in it.** That is what
`in_whole: false` is for: the whole test has to print without printing its own
answers, or a student sitting it can read the answers from the paper in front
of her. Prove it rather than assuming it — `pdftotext 00-lesson.pdf -` and grep
for a phrase that appears only in the key, and also confirm
`06-answer-key.pdf` exists on its own and does contain it.

Then read the rest with your own eyes: the listening sheet, the reading
passage (insertion markers printed), Section 3, and the key's tables. Check no
page break lands inside a question, and that the pauses in the listening audio
are long enough to answer in.
